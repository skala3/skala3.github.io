Memorization on Fine-Tuned LLMs

Quantifying extraction and privacy risks in LoRA fine-tuning, evaluating Goldfish and introducing Surgeonfish defenses.

LoRA FT MIA & Extraction Goldfish / Surgeonfish

Project Overview

Systematic study of memorization in LoRA fine-tuning: measuring syntactic memorization and membership inference leakage across LoRA ranks, training epochs, and mitigation strategies. Implements Goldfish loss and proposes Surgeonfish / Surgeonfish+ to suppress verbatim copying while preserving task utility.

Experiments span extraction attacks, naïve and shadow-model MIAs, perplexity gap analysis, and mitigation effectiveness. Findings show memorization rises with training duration, is weakly affected by LoRA rank, and that syntactic mitigations alone do not fully prevent privacy leakage.

Key Findings

Training Duration Drives Risk

Memorization and MIA leakage grow with epochs; LoRA rank has minimal effect.

Syntactic vs Privacy

Syntactic memorization remains low, yet shadow-model MIAs reach high AUC early.

Mitigation Limits

Goldfish/Surgeonfish reduce verbatim copying but only modestly lower MIA leakage.

Results & Notes

Syntactic Memorization

ROUGE-L, Exact Match, Levenshtein stay low through 10 epochs; rank has little effect.

Membership Inference

Shadow MIAs reach >0.9 AUC by epoch 3; naïve MIAs climb steadily to ~0.8 by epoch 10.

Perplexity Gap

Member perplexity drops far below non-member by epoch 10, signaling confidence overfitting.

Mitigation Impact

Surgeonfish+ best reduces syntactic copying; MIAs only slightly improved—privacy needs stronger defenses.
